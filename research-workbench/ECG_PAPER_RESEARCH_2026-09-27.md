# ECG project: paper lineage and research directions (27 September 2026)

## What the old project actually cites or uses

- The local [notebook](</Users/gyklty/Desktop/Guy/Job - Guy/old/Projects/realtime-ecg-arrhythmia-classification/ECG_MITBIH.ipynb>) loads `mitbih_train.csv` and `mitbih_test.csv`, each with 187 waveform values and an N/S/V/F/Q label. These are the [Kaggle ECG Heartbeat Categorization files](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/shayanfazeli/heartbeat), derived from MIT-BIH and associated with [Kachuee, Fazeli, and Sarrafzadeh (2018)](https://arxiv.org/html/1805.00794v2). The paper resampled lead II to 125 Hz, used a 1D residual CNN for five AAMI beat categories, and explored transfer of that representation to a **different** PTB myocardial-infarction task. Thus Kachuee is a defensible *data and task provenance*, but the notebook is not a reproduction of that paper's architecture or transfer experiment.
- The notebook includes a later CNN + channel-attention + Transformer block and a section labeled "SMOTE-Tomek + Undersample." The code in that section imports and calls `SMOTE` and undersamples N; it does **not** call `TomekLinks` or `SMOTETomek`. I found no occurrence of "CAT-Net," "Kachuee," the CAT-Net DOI, or a bibliography in notebook source cells or in text extracted from the local [presentation](</Users/gyklty/Desktop/Guy/Job - Guy/old/Projects/realtime-ecg-arrhythmia-classification/ECG_Arrhythmia_Classification_with_Real-time_Hospital_Monitoring_Final.pdf>). The resemblance to CAT-Net is architectural evidence only; we should call it *CAT-Net-like*, not a verified reproduction. This conclusion is limited to these local artifacts.
- The notebook applies `SMOTE` before its train/validation split in an early branch (cells 13 and 25). Synthetic examples can therefore share information across that split. Its CSV rows do not carry record or patient IDs, so a patient-wise split cannot be established from those files alone. The old results remain useful historical beat-level results, not evidence of unseen-patient performance.

## Foundational sources

| Source | What it actually adds | Protocol boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Kachuee et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/html/1805.00794v2) | Residual 1D CNN on MIT-BIH five-class beat classification; transfer of learned ECG features to PTB MI classification. | Reports 93.4% average arrhythmia accuracy on a balanced test of 4,079 beats. This is not directly comparable with accuracy on the Kaggle imbalanced held-out CSV. |
| [Islam et al. CAT-Net 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1746809424002696) | CNN local features, channel attention, Transformer context, comparison of SMOTE-ENN/SMOTE-Tomek/ADASYN; single-lead five-class MIT-BIH and three-class INCART. | Reports 99.14% accuracy / 94.69% macro F1 on MIT-BIH, 99.58% / 96.15% on INCART. Those are paper-reported results; we have not verified a patient-disjoint MIT-BIH protocol or reproduced them. CAT-Net still reports weaker F-class performance. |
| [PhysioNet MIT-BIH](https://physionet.org/content/mitdb/1.0.0/) | 48 half-hour, two-channel records from 47 subjects, roughly 110,000 annotated beats; record identity retained in raw data. | Suitable source for an auditable record/patient split. The source ECG is sampled at 360 Hz; the Kaggle derivative is at 125 Hz. |
| [PhysioNet INCART](https://physionet.org/content/incartdb/1.0.0/) | 75 half-hour, 12-lead records from 32 Holter recordings, more than 175,000 annotated beats; headers map records to patient numbers. | External dataset useful for testing transport. Keep label map and lead choice fixed and disclose that three-class INCART and five-class MIT-BIH are different tasks. |

## Later papers that explicitly engage with CAT-Net

1. [Rodriguez and Nafea, *Hierarchical Attention Network for Interpretable ECG-based Heart Disease Classification* (2025 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03703) **reimplements and directly compares** CAT-Net on MIT-BIH under shared preprocessing and 30 epochs. Its two-level hierarchical attention model has 76,265 parameters versus CAT-Net's 1,189,637 and reports 98.55% versus 99.14% test accuracy. It adds a model-size/interpretability tradeoff and attention visualization. It also adapts both models to PTB-XL, but that is a different label/task setup, so its PTB-XL figures must stay separate. Attention visualization alone is not proof of faithful explanation. The paper does not establish a patient-disjoint MIT-BIH comparison in the text inspected.
2. [Qin et al., *ECG beat classification based on dilated multi-scale ResNet and BiLSTM* (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6501/ae8615) **cites CAT-Net and develops an alternative architecture**: multi-scale residual convolutions, dilation for larger receptive field, and BiLSTM. The paper's abstract specifies a patient-wise MIT-BIH split, SMOTE on training data after splitting, ablations, and external INCART validation. This advances evaluation design as well as architecture. It reports 99.39% MIT-BIH and 98.75% INCART accuracy; these cannot be ranked against CAT-Net's values without matching beat labels, patient partition, preprocessing and lead. The publisher full text was inaccessible via browsing; the abstract and citation relationship were cross-checked through the paper record, so detailed implementation claims need full-text verification before reproduction.
3. [Almasoud et al., *An explainable deep learning framework for trustworthy arrhythmia detection* (2025)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-22986-0) **cites and compares published CAT-Net figures** while adding random oversampling and SHAP/LIME/feature-importance displays around a small 1D CNN. Crucial caveat: this paper maps MIT-BIH beats into **binary** normal/abnormal classes; its 99.74% accuracy is therefore not a like-for-like improvement over CAT-Net's five-class number. It demonstrates a possible explanation/imbalance branch, not a valid CAT-Net benchmark win.

## Additional newer work worth testing, without claiming direct descent

- [HAT-ECG (2026)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42338216/) combines convolutional autoencoder representations with a Transformer and reports both beat-wise and patient-wise MIT-BIH evaluation. Its own 99.91% beat-wise versus 90.81% patient-wise accuracy illustrates how much the split changes the question. It also studies INCART and PTB, with different class/task definitions. This is strong motivation for our evaluation protocol, not an interchangeable CAT-Net score.
- [Diao et al. (September 2026)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1746809426011353) uses multi-beat T-to-T context, multi-scale convolution, squeeze-and-excitation channel attention, and focal loss plus label smoothing. Its abstract claims strict inter-patient MIT-BIH evaluation and INCART validation. This suggests that neighboring beats and rhythm context may address N/S confusion beyond a single 187-point beat. Do not adopt its headline 99.64% as a comparison until its patient IDs, AAMI mappings and test composition are checked.

## Recommended Guy-owned research contribution

Rebuild the data pipeline from [raw MIT-BIH](https://physionet.org/content/mitdb/1.0.0/) so every beat retains `record_id`, source patient where known, source sampling rate and preprocessing version. Freeze patient-disjoint development/test partitions *before* scaling, augmentation or class balancing. Evaluate a simple 1D CNN, a carefully reproduced CAT-Net-like model, and one compact model inspired by the later efficiency work under **the same** data and metrics. Report macro F1, N/S/V/F/Q per-class precision/recall, patient-level spread, calibration and latency; keep INCART as a separately mapped external test.

A distinct project contribution could be **shift-aware selective beat classification**: add an explicit signal-quality / unfamiliar-patient score and allow abstention when the model lacks reliable evidence, then measure risk versus coverage for S/F beats under unseen patients and added noise. This is a *proposal*, not a novel-in-literature claim. It should be tested against confidence-only abstention under the same fixed split. This addresses the old project's hardest failure mode more directly than another UI or higher beat-level accuracy figure.

This paper review did not run training or use the cluster. The separate implementation stage subsequently downloaded and verified the MIT-BIH core files for a raw annotation inventory; see [implementation/README.md](implementation/README.md).
