# ECG data expansion: source audit and recommendation

30 September 2026. Planning research only: no signals, annotations, models, or sealed SVDB material were downloaded or inspected; no jobs were run. Claims below use the dataset owners' PhysioNet release pages and documentation.

## Current question

The active project predicts **N/S/V/F for annotated beats** using raw MIT-BIH with subject-disjoint development partitions. INCART has been used for development transport checks; SVDB remains sealed. Recent [longer-run experiments](ECG_LONGER_RESULT_2026-09-30.md) have not reliably solved rare F detection. Adding recordings is useful only if the new source has compatible *beat-level* labels and independent people, or if we define a separate pretraining/diagnostic task.

The [v1.0.3 page](https://physionet.org/content/ptb-xl/1.0.3/) gives 21,799 / 18,869 and documents removal of duplicate waveforms in v1.0.2/v1.0.3. Pin the version and use its own metadata; do not mix headline counts from another release.

## Labels, splits, and overlap

- **EDB mapping for an exploratory common beat task:** `NORMAL → N`; `ABERR`, `NPC`, `SVPB → S`; `PVC → V`; `FUSION → F`. Exclude `UNKNOWN`, artifact and non-beat annotations unless a separately specified task needs them. This is a proposed operational mapping of the [EDB code definitions](https://physionet.org/physiobank/database/edb/annotations.shtml), not proof that every class has adequate support. Confirm any annotation convention differences before training. Avoid silently treating ST/T episode labels as arrhythmia beat labels.
- **EDB patient split:** the [official description](https://physionet.org/content/edb/1.0.0/) explicitly identifies several groups of records from the same person (for example e0118–e0122 and e0123–e0126). Group those records together, use the full header/record relationship inventory, and retain the source dataset/record ID with every extracted beat. A record-random split would leak people. Age/sex availability is incomplete for one subject; patient IDs across separate datasets are not harmonized.
- **PTB-XL** already has `patient_id` and recommended `strat_fold`; folds 1–8/9/10 are proposed for train/validation/test in its own record-level task. **Chapman/Shaoxing/Ningbo** documents patient ECG IDs but does not supply a comparable official fold assignment on its release page. Preserve patient IDs; verify whether multiple records or republished versions share waveforms before splitting or combining releases.
- **QTDB is unsuitable as a fresh external source:** the [QT Database](https://physionet.org/content/qtdb/1.0.0/) contains excerpts from other databases. Its [official provenance table](https://physionet.org/physiobank/database/qtdb/doc/node3.html) counts **15 MIT-BIH Arrhythmia** and **33 European ST-T** source records, among others. QTDB adds waveform-boundary annotations; it does not create independent people. Do not use it to enlarge or confirm a beat classifier trained on MIT-BIH/EDB without an explicit origin-level exclusion list. Likewise, do not double-count the 10,646-record Chapman Figshare predecessor alongside its PhysioNet successor.
- **Cross-dataset independence:** EDB's European collection and PTB-XL's German diagnostic collection are described separately from MIT-BIH's Boston cohort, but no cross-dataset patient identity guarantee or waveform duplicate audit is published on these pages. Keep a source provenance manifest, check any known reuse, and perform waveform/record collision checks before claiming independent confirmation. Do not inspect or match against sealed SVDB while it remains sealed.

## Recommendation

**First, assess EDB for the existing beat question.** Its true beat labels, including `FUSION`, and 79 subjects make it more relevant than the much larger record-labeled datasets. Before any EDB training, inspect only its public headers and `.atr` annotations to produce an exact N/S/V/F count **by subject**, lead availability table, mapping exclusions and source hashes. Then decide whether F appears across enough independent subjects to support a meaningful training and held-out evaluation. If F is again concentrated in one or two people, more total beats will not solve the specific generalization problem. Preserve MIT-BIH development scores as development evidence; make an explicit decision about the role of EDB (training source or untouched confirmation) *before* viewing its model scores.

**Second, if storage permits, choose one separate large-scale branch:** PTB-XL v1.0.3 is the cleaner first choice because it provides patient IDs, patient-respecting folds, label hierarchy, signal-quality metadata and a fixed version. Use its record-level diagnoses for a distinct diagnostic task or its ECGs for self-supervised pretraining, then test whether pretraining changes the same frozen MIT-BIH/EDB beat protocol. Chapman/Shaoxing/Ningbo has more patients but an added 5.1 GB uncompressed and no per-beat target labels; it is an alternative or later scale-up, not an automatic improvement to the current classifier.

**Resource boundary:** the three new releases would occupy about **8.6 GB uncompressed** in total before extracted arrays, checkpoints, caches, duplicate copies or backups (3.0 + 5.1 GB + 0.465 GB, using official rounded figures). The immediate EDB annotation/header inventory can be planned without downloading signal files. Storage quota is pending, so bulk signal transfer and training should be sized only after that quota and the annotation counts are known. No clinical deployment claim follows from any of these datasets.
