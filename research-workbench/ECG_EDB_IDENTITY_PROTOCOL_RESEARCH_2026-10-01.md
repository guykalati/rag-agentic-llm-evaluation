# EDB identity and recording-overlap protocol — source review (2026-10-01)

## What the primary sources establish

- **European ST-T Database (EDB 1.0.0):** 90 two-hour, two-lead ambulatory ECG excerpts from 79 subjects. The [release page](https://physionet.org/content/edb/1.0.0/) explicitly names seven sets of records that share a subject. The [original dataset paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1396824/) says 13 research groups in eight countries supplied the tapes; Pisa and Rotterdam coordinated the database. It also says MIT helped make the *annotation scheme* consistent with MIT-BIH. Shared authorship, file format, and annotation conventions do **not** establish shared recordings or patients. The [original EDB documentation](https://physionet.org/files/edb/1.0.0/edb.txt) describes header demographics, leads, clinical findings, and equipment, but gives no identifier that can be joined to another database's patient ID.
- **MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database 1.0.0:** 48 half-hour excerpts from 47 subjects studied by the BIH Arrhythmia Laboratory in 1975–1979; its source was a collection of about 4,000 Beth Israel Hospital Holter recordings in Boston. See the [official release description](https://physionet.org/content/mitdb/1.0.0/). **INCART 1.0.0:** 75 half-hour, 12-lead excerpts from 32 Holter records contributed by the St. Petersburg Institute of Cardiological Technics; its numbered patient groups identify multiple excerpts from the same source Holter. See the [official release description](https://physionet.org/content/incartdb/1.0.0/).
- The reviewed EDB, MIT-BIH, and INCART release descriptions and EDB paper **do not document a shared recording or a shared patient across these three releases**. This is absence of a documented link, **not proof of distinct patients**. A useful counterexample is the [QT Database's own source table](https://physionet.org/physiobank/database/qtdb/doc/node3.html), which explicitly lists excerpts from both MIT-BIH Arrhythmia and EDB. QTDB reuse demonstrates why derivative collections need source-record checks; it does not imply EDB and MIT-BIH duplicate each other.

## What a future overlap screen can establish

1. Preserve the EDB release's seven same-subject groups, MIT-BIH's documented shared-subject relationship, and INCART's header patient numbers before any split. For the current EDB **V5 subset**, the local [metadata inventory](EDB_METADATA_RESULT_2026-09-30.md) and current selection report 51 records in 51 documented EDB subject groups, with 343 F beats in 11 groups; the largest two groups dominate. “Documented group” is the correct term because cross-database identity remains unresolved. Keep SVDB sealed.
2. In subsequent waveform work, compare candidate EDB waveforms with existing MIT-BIH and INCART signals using aligned, sufficiently long ECG segments and consistent lead/scaling/resampling treatment. An exact signal-file hash catches only identical encodings; a near-duplicate search needs time offset and waveform/beat-timing checks, followed by manual review. Lead, sample-rate, gain, and excerpt-length differences can hide reuse from naive hashes. Record matched source IDs, leads, time interval, transformation, review evidence, and final exclusion. Do not infer a duplicate from common rhythm or a short similar beat alone.
3. A confirmed waveform match supports **same recorded episode**, so exclude or group it across partitions. A sufficiently sensitive negative screen supports only “no detected duplicate excerpt under this method.” It cannot show that two different ECG episodes came from different people. No shared cross-release patient identifier is provided in the reviewed documentation; only reliable source-custodian linkage could settle that identity question. Report any combined experiment as **subject-disjoint within each documented database and screened for detected recording duplication across databases**, with cross-database person overlap still unknown. Do not describe the combined corpus as proven patient-disjoint.
